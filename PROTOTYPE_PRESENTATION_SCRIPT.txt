Q1: "What if there are thick monsoon clouds or it is pitch black midnight?"
A1: "Optical sensors fail in clouds and darkness, which is why SpillSense relies on 
     Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) like Sentinel-1, ISRO EOS-04, and NISAR. SAR uses 
     active microwave pulses (C-band, S-band, L-band) that penetrate cloud cover, 
     monsoon rain, and darkness 24/7 without requiring any solar illumination."

Q2: "How do you differentiate crude oil from calm water or algal blooms?"
A2: "We use Dual-Frequency Bragg Scattering Analysis. Crude oil dampens both short 
     capillary ripples and longer gravity waves. Natural biogenic slicks (like algae) 
     only dampen short capillary waves. By computing the Dual-Frequency Damping Index 
     (DFDI) between NISAR S-band (3.2 GHz) and L-band (1.26 GHz), we separate mineral 
     petroleum from biogenic look-alikes with mathematical certainty."

Q3: "Where does the ocean current data come from for your drift predictions?"
A3: "Our Lagrangian engine ingests 3D hydrodynamic ocean currents from CMEMS 
     (Copernicus Marine Environment Monitoring Service) combined with 10-meter 
     surface wind vectors from ECMWF ERA5 reanalysis to model both advection and 
     Stokes drift."

Q4: "Can an offshore Coast Guard vessel run this without fast internet?"
A4: "Yes! Our ONNX WebAssembly model executes directly inside the browser using 
     the client laptop's CPU/GPU via WASM SIMD. No image bytes are sent over the network; 
     once the application is loaded, AI inference functions completely offline at sea."

Q5: "Why won't the drift projection penetrate into the mainland?"
A5: "We engineered a sovereign peninsular land clamping engine (geoContours.ts). 
     Every forward and backward trajectory waypoint is dynamically checked against 
     the Indian sovereign coastline baseline, mathematically clamping all dispersion 
     vectors strictly to navigable ocean waters."
